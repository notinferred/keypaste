# Q.9 — Let people enlarge text

Completed 2026-11-05 on `q9-enlarge-text`; ci 42003643681 passed its fast tier at the code integrated, which differs from it only in this record.

## Amendments

The founder decided, on selecting the row, that the minimum window scales with zoom but never passes the screen's working area, and content scrolls or reflows inside it, so "nothing cut off" at 200% on a small screen means every control is reachable. The row's Ctrl for other platforms comes from DESIGN.

On 2026-11-05 the founder decided the prompts' question: a prompt fits whole, as built, so on a screen too small for it a prompt stops short of 200%. BACKLOG holds a larger prompt layout for those who need 200% there.

Settled in the step, all reversible:

- The mechanism is Avalonia's `LayoutTransformControl`, in `ZoomFrame` for the main window and `PromptFrame` for a prompt, with `Popup.InheritsTransform` set by a style for flyouts, menus, combo boxes and tooltips. Avalonia has no per-window scale: `RenderScaling` comes from the platform, and `AVALONIA_GLOBAL_SCALE_FACTOR` is X11's and read at start. Font sizes alone would have left icons, spacing, targets and prompts' fixed heights behind, and every layout would have had to survive them. A layout transform redraws glyphs at the larger size through Skia, so text stays sharp, and hit testing, `GetPosition` and `TransformToVisual` already follow it, the divider's delta and `MaskedInput` included.
- `ZoomFrame`'s own scroller does not act on focus: `ScrollViewer` shows a focused control from every scroller on its route, and a second one moved a page that had already answered, 163px further on Env's add-an-item form. The frame moves only itself, and only while it scrolls.
- The body lays out at the design's pixels: `ShellView` measures itself inside the frame, so `NarrowBelow` needs no change and at 200% a window 1500 wide is narrow. It never lays out under 720×520 of those pixels; below that, on a screen that cannot hold the zoomed minimum, the whole frame scrolls, title bar included.
- Steps are 100, 125, 150, 175 and 200, and 100 is the floor: Zoom out and Actual size are off there, and Zoom in is off at 200. A window the zoom left under its minimum grows and stays on its screen; zooming out shrinks nothing.
- `zoom` sits in `[[window]]`, as a string (`"150%"`), and only away from 100%. A bare number is read too, and clamped. A bare negative is refused by the file's reader with the whole file, as for every number in it, which is why it is written as text. `zoom` beside bounds that do not read is ignored with them.
- The change is written as it happens, not only on close, since it is something the person asked for.
- A prompt takes the zoom while it lays out, up to the largest scale at which all of it fits its screen's working area, and holds that once it has opened, so zooming from the main window moves nothing under the pointer. **On a screen too small for a prompt at the zoom, rule 8 wins over rule 7's 200%**: on 1366×768 at Windows' 150% the tallest prompt draws at 0.64, because the alternative hides Deny or the reason behind a scroll. DESIGN's rules 7 and 8 now say so.
- Masked values: `RevealedValue` now defaults to 14 and no view overrides it. The current password was at 13, a history revision at 12 and an env value at 12.5, under the 14 the row's acceptance holds.
- View-level `FontSize` attributes, 185 of them, became classes in a new `TextSizes.axaml` of size-only classes, included last and again at the end of `PromptStyles.axaml`, because classes that also set weight or colour would have changed those. `11.5`, off the scale, moved to 12 on 11 lines. Setters inside a view's own `<Style>` and `PromptStyles.axaml` keep their sizes: they are already named rules, and moving them shifted nothing a person enlarges. `first-text` and `terms-heading` gave up their setters because they overrode a class on the element.
- `AmberElements` grows its join distances and minimum area with the zoom, and takes control bounds through the transform, so a line of amber text is one element at any size.

Defects the reach walk found at 100% and the step fixed:

- `ScrollViewer.Padding` does not narrow what it measures, so wrapped text ran past the right edge and was cut: an access-change prompt read "recorded in hist". All seven such scrollers in the prompts now give the margin to their content.
- Env's row actions, 8 apart since Q.8, need 120 and had 112, cutting Remove by 8px. The column is 120.
- The share dialog's placeholder was wider than its field; it now reads "At least 20 characters, such as six generated words".
- `Back` lay over the page by 16px with a transparent ground, so a scrolled page showed through it. It now has a row of its own that the page's viewport starts below, and the screens under a place keep 16 less at their top, so their layout stays put when not scrolled.
- On Env's matrix a masked value lay over a note that wrapped under it, at a design width near 720: the mask was centred in the cell. Main's sizes (12.5 and a 112 column) do the same at 100% and 720 in this tree's test, so it is older than Q.9, from Q.10's width. Every line of the cell is now as tall as the mask, which sits on the first. One-line cells stay put; a wrapped cell is 4 taller.
- Import's dialog could run off a short window; it scrolls. Trash's caption pushed its buttons past the edge; it sits under them. The tokens table's Mode and Expires columns have minimum widths.

## Limits and follow-ups

The 100% comparison above predates the last two repairs and its noise mask predates the back link's row, so its count of matching frames may run high; Env's one-line cells have since shifted by about a pixel, and it was not rerun.

No person has used this, and no real window was opened: the built app could not start its render timer (`-6661`) because the display was asleep, which main does too and BACKLOG now holds, so `screen.Scaling`, the title bar hint at zoom, the traffic lights and caption buttons, a real `PopupRoot`, and macOS's handling of a `Key.OemPlus` key equivalent are unverified on every platform. Headless popups are overlays inside the window, so their scale was tested through the same `Transform` a `PopupRoot` takes, not in one. Another keyboard layout's plus is not tested. At 150% on a 1× screen a 1px line is 1.5 pixels and antialiased; text is not.

The title bar scrolls with the rest when the frame scrolls, so a window on a small screen can be dragged only from the top. The desktop gates, `compat` and the upgrade gate were not run locally.

BACKLOG's investigation candidates gain the menu's chord text on Windows and Linux.
